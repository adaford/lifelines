## The first playable framework

- First name and family surname are optional; blank fields use random defaults, and a supplied first name is preserved. A typed surname's first letter is capitalized automatically. The story starts at age 0 with a female Mom and male Dad, distinct first names from gendered name pools, inherited natural aptitudes, and a growing height. Sons and daughters keep the whole family surname, including multiword names.
- Babies and adults share intelligence, strength, attractiveness, social, and creativity ratings from 0–10. Before 18, abilities compare with others of the same age: strength 7 means a strong baby, not adult strength. The four trainable ratings start around 5, influenced by inherited natural aptitude from −3 to +3 and random variation; a gifted infant can start high. Attractiveness begins near its inherited mature level and does not gain ordinary growth from crying, focus, or careers.
- All seven rated stats can begin anywhere from 0–10, with broad random variation around 5. Health and mental stability start from `5 + 0.6 * aptitude` plus normal variation with SD 2, bounded to 0-10. Higher mental stability means calmer and more resilient; low mental stability increases the chances of impulsive behavior, conflict, and distress. Newborn averages remain near 5, and childhood wellbeing retains its fitted targets. With the requested 90% reduction in background skill deductions, trainable-skill averages may rise above 5 by age 18.
- Until age 18, lifestyle matches the parent household. Both parents share a household lifestyle that changes with their career focus, progress, promotions, job losses, and recovery. Career growth generally improves it over time, but setbacks remain possible. Children you raise share yours. At 18, career success and personal resources determine lifestyle.
- Ages 0–1: choose a cry level from 1–5. Less crying lowers intelligence and mental stability; more crying raises them. Crying does not affect strength. Parent relationships tend to fall at levels 1–2, remain steady at 3, and rise at 4–5. Harder crying increases parent stress. Background development and yearly events can change the final outcome.
- Ages 2-4: share 10 base activity points among Play, Electronics, Family time, Rest, and Arts & crafts. Each starts at 2 and can range from 0 to 4. Play develops strength and social; Electronics develops intelligence with a smaller social cost; Family time supports social, caregiver mental stability, and caregiver relationships; Arts & crafts develops creativity and intelligence; Rest supports wellbeing.
- School begins at 5: share 10 base focus points. School, Friends, Family, and Yourself use 0-4 points, initially 2. Sports and Extracurriculars stay at 0 until enrollment, then use 0-2 points, initially 1. Each category offers five annual choices; GPA and activity success update in the yearly report card. College and adults keep four focuses and an eight-point budget; birth conditions can change points or impose lower caps. Unused points stay unspent, without automatic rest gains.
- Advance exactly one year per click. Toddler, School, and College effects appear within each choice row; infancy and adult focus use You, Other people, and Relationships groups. Ordinary focus effects use one sign at 1/3 points and three at 0/4; crafts Intelligence uses one sign only at 0/4; combined adult totals can use up to six. Signs scale with actual raw contributions; crying retains its existing relative scale. Zero changes and empty groups are omitted. A year-review popup shows actual before/after values and events, surviving reloads until dismissed.
- 309 personal and household random events each roll independently, with a maximum of five per year. Events now occur in most years under typical balanced conditions, while no-event years remain possible. Your age, school/college status, career, focus, and effective stats shape which events can happen and their odds. Seventeen childhood parent events include comfort, routines, stress spilling into home life, illness, and help with caregiving; parents' own wellbeing and relationships affect the chances.
- 73 events pause play for a decision. Each option shows its permanent stat changes and any temporary activity limits. Choices and outcomes persist through reloads and appear in the journal. No yearly advance or career/family change can skip a pending choice.
- A pool of 100 distinct careers spans 10 fields. Every job has skill affinities, a base lifestyle (0–10), and age/skill requirements. This is the only financial number shown: no salary, income, spending, savings, or inheritance amounts appear in the game.
- At 18, choose college or take a job from 10 offers. Choosing a job starts the workforce path; advancing is paused until the choice is made.
- College takes four yearly turns. College focus develops skills while studying; graduating opens a new list of 15 offers, including degree-based careers. The game remembers each list rather than rerolling it when a menu opens.
- Single adults can meet up to five people per year. Attractiveness, social, and lifestyle affect frequency, profile strength, and pursuit success. Profiles show every stat, affinity, and birth condition; browse one at a time on a phone. Choose a relationship, a consensual one-night stand, or ignore. Unanswered encounters expire the following year.
- A new relationship starts at a random 3–7 out of 10. Dating adds social +0.7 and mental stability +0.6; marriage raises these to +0.9 and +0.8. Either costs one lifestyle level. Family focus builds the relationship. Propose once per year; relationship strength and years together determine acceptance.
- Dating has a low annual pregnancy chance; marriage raises it substantially. Pregnancy is saved, and a baby arrives on the next yearly turn. A one-night stand also has a small pregnancy chance. Adults can adopt, with a maximum of six children and one arrival per year. An expected baby reserves a place in the family.
- Each living dependent child under 18 adds social +0.2 (up to +1.2) and costs one lifestyle level. Children have their own 0–10 relationship, stats, and affinities. Family focus improves their relationship and abilities. Partners and children have personal events and decisions with clearly labeled consequences for them and for you.
- Attractiveness starts near its inherited mature value and fluctuates slightly in either direction each year. Its mean stays flat until gradual decline begins from age 30 to 31 for men and age 22 to 23 for women; other genders currently use 30. This is appearance pacing for the game, not a scientific attractiveness standard. Specific events can still change appearance.
- Everyone in the active family can die in any year, including the player, parents, partner, children, and siblings. Age supplies a real-world baseline; lower health raises disease risk. Death rolls are separate from the five ordinary events.
- Retirement opens at 65, and death opens succession at any age. Choose any eligible child; if there are no eligible children, choose a sibling. With neither, give the legacy to a random child aged 6–12 with natural ratings and a different surname. At 85, retirement from the playable chapter is required; it does not kill the retiring character. Family members can live beyond 85. The heir preview stays the same across menu visits and reloads.
- Heirs keep their age, stats, affinity, birth conditions, height, identity, and personal resources. Child and sibling heirs keep your family surname regardless of gender. A sibling handoff preserves the existing parents and remaining siblings; a previously played character cannot receive another handoff. Newly born/adopted children record known parent IDs, allowing the current partner to remain a parent after a child handoff when that relationship is recorded. Old saves do not guess unknown parentage.
- The entire estate is divided equally among eligible living children, including those not selected as the next player. With no children, the chosen sibling or unrelated child receives the whole estate. Inheritance supports their lifestyle, and the history records previous chapters.
- Automatic local browser saves, JSON export/import, keyboard-accessible controls, and responsive layouts.

## Birth conditions

The catalog in [birth-conditions.js](birth-conditions.js) contains 100 good, bad, and mixed birth conditions. New players, parents, guardians, children, dating prospects, and unrelated heirs receive zero to five distinct conditions. Any condition can occur without either parent having it, regardless of gender. Every entry has a positive base chance (usually 1.2%; Autism uses 0.8%). Either biological parent having a condition multiplies its roll chance by 1.5; both parents do not compound the boost. Adoption uses independent rolls. Candidates are shuffled before the five-condition cap, and mutually exclusive catalog groups cannot occur together. These are fictional game mechanics.

Condition affinity changes apply once at creation after ratings are generated, summed by stat and bounded to integer -3 through +3. They never change ratings directly. Point adjustments and stage-specific caps last throughout life. High energy grants +1 yearly focus point; Autism grants +1 Intelligence affinity and caps Friends at 1 from age 5. Point adjustments sum with the normal 10-point toddler/school or 8-point adult budget; the most restrictive condition or temporary event cap wins. Conditions persist through aging, relationships, reloads, and succession without repeating affinity changes. Separate perk rows with one effect per line appear on Year, family, dating, and heir profiles.

Schema 17 stores condition IDs on every person. Existing people in older saves receive empty lists without rolls or stat bonuses. Active focus adapts deterministically to the new 0-4 limits and budget; ratings, aptitudes, RNG, pending decisions, effects, pregnancy, and history stay intact. Older reviews retain recorded choices and former limits; new reviews store their maximum and budget.

Base personal ratings, relationships, and lifestyle range from **0.0 to 10.0**. Temporary player stat/lifestyle modifiers are added afterward without clamping, so the displayed total may go below 0 or above 10. Existing saves keep their exact values; the wider range applies to subsequent development and events.

## Source and deployment

- `engine.js`: deterministic simulation, surnames, college, job offers, events, saves, and retirement inheritance.
- `development.js`: natural starting ratings, lifelong focus/aptitude development, childhood wellbeing mean reversion, and pure infant choice contributions.
- `choice-impacts.js`: grouped, relative choice previews with no save or RNG mutation.
- `percentiles.js`: normal CDF for percentile calculations.
- `percentile-reference.js`: generated same-age cohort references and reproduction metadata.
- `scripts/calibrate-percentiles.mjs`: offline development fitting and reference generation.
- `parent-careers.js`: parent career progression, employment events, and household lifestyle.
- `jobs.js`: 100 career titles, skill affinities, lifestyle ratings, and entry requirements.
- `events.js`: personal events, cross-stat aptitude experiences, age/context eligibility, and independent probabilities.
- `rating-changes.js`: shared directional rating-change multiplier and bounded two-decimal application.
- `family-events.js`: parent/partner/child events with person-specific odds and permanent outcomes.
- `mortality.js`: sourced age-specific baseline, stat modifiers, and one annual competing-risk death roll.
- `growth.js`: WHO/CDC annual growth references, NPC height percentiles, maturation, and annual variation.
- `relationships.js`: dating, pursuit, marriage, pregnancy, and attractiveness-aging rules.
- `app.js`: UI, actions, local persistence, and import/export.
- `display.js`: one-decimal stat ratings and age-aware height display formatting.
- `narrative.js`: relationship annotations for known names in rendered stories.
- `styles.css`: responsive layout and visual design.
- `index.html` and `favicon.svg`: static entry point and branding.
- `test/`: engine and DOM integration tests.

This uses the same deployment pattern as **Depths**: a separate public GitHub repository, GitHub Pages serving the root of `main`, and `.nojekyll`. Pushes to `main` automatically redeploy.

```sh
git add app.js choice-impacts.js display.js engine.js development.js percentiles.js percentile-reference.js parent-careers.js narrative.js scripts jobs.js events.js family-events.js relationships.js mortality.js growth.js styles.css index.html favicon.svg README.md HANDOFF.md AGENTS.md test package.json package-lock.json .gitignore .nojekyll server.mjs
git commit -m "Describe the change"
git push origin main
```

Check deployment with `gh api repos/adaford/lifelines/pages/builds/latest`. Game progress stays in the player’s browser, not in GitHub. Exports can move a save between devices; the prototype has no accounts or cloud synchronization.
