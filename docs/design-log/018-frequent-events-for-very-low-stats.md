# Frequent events for very low stats (11 November 2026)

Very low ratings now produce related events roughly every other year. The pure shared `event-rates.js` helper raises the combined probability of at least one eligible ordinary low-stat event to at least 50% at rating 2, 55% at 1, and 60% at 0. The added probability fades toward no adjustment at 4. Existing higher combined rates are never lowered. Player calculations use effective ratings, including Lifestyle; family calculations use each named person's own ratings.

Only non-rare, non-major low-stat events receive the increase. Major crises, rare aptitude events, abandonment, and unrelated/high-stat event rates retain their previous probabilities. Boosted ordinary events may reach 60% individually when a stat has few eligible incidents; this supersedes the prior 18% personal/12% household cap only for that adjustment. All other caps remain. Use a shared hazard multiplier within each stat's eligible pool; an event linked to several low stats takes the strongest multiplier, never their product. Eligibility, including school-grade gates, runs first. Rolls remain independent and share the five-event yearly maximum, so several simultaneous low stats can compete for those slots.

Six minor automatic encounters fill early-childhood Intelligence, Social, Creativity, Attractiveness, and Lifestyle gaps plus later-life Lifestyle. They describe confusing play, an unfamiliar visitor, a changed game, an unkind appearance remark, unavailable playgroup access, and missing project materials. Each uses a low-stat factor and a maximum rating of 4; permanent cross-stat losses are .1-.15 before directional response and bounds. The catalog now has 352 definitions and 73 decisions. No saved stats, focus, effects, decisions, history, RNG, or schema change on load; schema remains 20.

Validation: all 526 tests pass on Node 24.14.0, including 116 DOM checks and 21,800 sampled yearly advances. With one rating held at 1, ordinary related personal events occur in 52.0-58.3% of sampled years after the five-event maximum; named-parent mental-stability events occur in 54.6-57.4%. Existing higher teen/adult mental-stability rates remain higher. All 25 local HTTP assets match source. The 4,000-life percentile reference was regenerated without fitting and reproduces exactly; the independent 1,600-life cohort matches every mean within .079. More frequent consequences lower achieved age-18 mental stability to about 4.07 while the underlying birth distributions and routine wellbeing targets remain unchanged. Preserve these consequences rather than refitting them away. Browser/visual checks remain omitted by preference. Cache token: `low-stat-events`.
